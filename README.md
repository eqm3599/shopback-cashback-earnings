## What cashback actually means

Cashback is a rebate connected to a qualifying purchase. A retailer pays a commission when a customer arrives through a tracked referral link. The cashback platform keeps part of that commission and passes a portion back to the shopper.

For example, imagine a store displays a 5% cashback offer and you place a qualifying $100 order. The basic calculation would be:

- Order value: $100
- Cashback rate: 5%
- Potential cashback: $5

That does not necessarily mean the final cashback will be exactly $5. Retailers may calculate cashback using the eligible merchandise subtotal while excluding taxes, shipping, gift cards, selected brands, subscriptions, or other restricted items. ShopBack’s own tracking guide says that store-specific terms control how cashback is calculated, and many stores do not include shipping or taxes in the qualifying amount.

The rate can also change. A store may show a standard rate most of the time and a higher boosted rate during a limited campaign. Some offers use a flat dollar amount instead of a percentage. The store page inside ShopBack is the place to check the live terms before ordering.

## How much cashback can you earn?

There is no single cashback rate across ShopBack. The amount depends on the retailer, product category, promotion, customer eligibility, and the store’s exclusions.

A realistic way to evaluate an offer is to check three things:

### The headline rate

This is the percentage or dollar amount displayed on the retailer page. “Up to” rates need a closer look because the maximum may apply only to selected categories or new customers.

### The eligible purchase amount

A 5% offer may apply to the merchandise subtotal rather than the complete amount charged to your card. Taxes, delivery charges, gift cards, marketplace items, and certain brands can be excluded.

### The confirmation conditions

An offer may require a completed purchase, a minimum order value, a new customer status, a specific payment method, or a purchase that is not returned. Read the retailer’s terms before assuming the headline amount is guaranteed.

For example, the difference between a $100 order with a 5% rate and a $100 order with only $60 of eligible merchandise is significant:

- 5% of $100: $5
- 5% of $60: $3

The checkout total may look identical in both cases, but the cashback calculation is different.

## Is ShopBack cashback real money?

ShopBack describes its US cashback as withdrawable money rather than points or store credit. The available withdrawal methods are PayPal and ACH transfer, subject to account verification, confirmation status, and the applicable minimum balance.

The catch is that cashback is conditional. It is tied to a qualifying transaction and must survive the merchant’s validation process. Cashback may be reduced or cancelled when:

- The order is returned.
- The order is cancelled.
- A partial refund changes the eligible value.
- The purchase uses an unsupported coupon.
- The product category is excluded.
- Another referral source receives the transaction.
- Tracking fails during checkout.
- A travel reservation is cancelled or not completed.

So, yes, the money is real. It is simply not immediate. Think of pending cashback as a record of expected earnings rather than cash that is already available to withdraw.
